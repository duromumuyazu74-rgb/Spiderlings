# Runtime ownership

Module state and test-environment contracts are maintained in [MODULES.md](MODULES.md). The manifest owns script loading order. Runtime scripts remain plain JavaScript in KD's native global environment, sharing the `Spiderlings` namespace.

`SpiderlingsFieldCustody.js` owns persistent captured-prey attribution and its bounded interception/contact team. It receives actual Capture and completed-return events, survives temporary controller teardown, and resolves competing NPC escorts into explicit attack targets. NativeActions and Duties execute those field decisions through native movement and combat. Player recovery requires a saved actual field exit while wearing the leg bag; interior movement and bag possession alone do not qualify. Recovery contact cannot be vetoed solely by native `ignoretiedup`; unrelated autonomous encounters retain that policy. Nearby eligible idle Spinners are assigned through FieldCommand, preserving active sources and original homes. [MODULES.md](MODULES.md) defines the interfaces and cache invalidation rules.

`SpiderlingsFieldCommand.js` owns the map-local command ledger, donor approval, temporary loans, returns and non-Spinner region orders. `SpiderlingsFieldProjects.js` owns continuous coverage/demand assessment, persistent project selection, construction reservations, maintenance admission, rally positions and work execution. Duties reads detached work snapshots; Command closes requests through its demand transitions. NativeField owns structure replacement and rollback. `SpiderlingsSpinnerDuties.js` shares one authoritative duty across movement, attacks and spells.

FieldProjects permits new independent fields only below both `floor(FieldCommand.spinnerCount() / 4)` and the configured maximum. Living eligible hostile Spinners count even while temporarily incapacitated or committed elsewhere. Allies, party and imprisoned actors are excluded. Mapgen and replacement deployments use the same gates; existing fields retain their investment after a population or setting reduction. Authored diagnostic line fixtures retain their explicit test catalogue and do not represent independent capture-field deployment.

A character within four reachable steps of an outer boundary requests readiness staffing during that positive-time planning pass. Entry into the outer area increases urgency; only core entry requests Capture. Demand urgency is independent of work kind. Safe lower-priority loans can transfer directly from travel, support or return, retaining their original home and one current commander. A same-field duty change reuses the loan, while the request identity invalidates stale action decisions. Actual sources remain protected and count only for their matching field and target.

Support reports distinguish deployed promises, physical arrival, usable staff, incoming helpers and obstruction. An unmanned ready geometry does not provide operational coverage. Friendly mobile spiders on unfinished work cells can spend their own native movement operation to yield; committed spells, dashes, channels and real control sources keep their duties. With no executable work assignment, a Spinner may pass a genuinely perceived hostile obstruction to native combat. Occupied construction and worker shortages remain separate facts, and every movement, attack and work action retains native payment.

Core precedes NativeActions; NativeActions precedes species registration and the late SpinnerRuntime installer. `SpiderlingsSettings.js` loads after Encounters and presents the existing settings catalogue on general, floors and nests pages without changing its saved namespace. `SpiderlingsBestiary.js` loads after enemy registration and uses `KDNewLore` for discovery, native Journal rendering and native persistence. Webbing data precedes Webbing rules, which precede the native Webbing adapter. Tests explicitly load these dependencies; the independent manifest allowlist test protects delivery order. Rule-only tests load data and rules without registering enemies, items, native hooks or game events.

FieldProjects submits maintenance demand independently of capture demand when protected sources occupy its crew. FieldCommand refreshes travelling destinations from the continuing request while retaining the loan identity. NativeField projects unactivated field silk as player-passable structures through `KDCanPassEnemy` and adds a one-turn `SlowLevel: 2` buff in `beforeMove`. Native movement owns the extra turn. The optional saved field flag `silkActivated` changes only through successful paid closure/reopening; older sealed or breached fields infer activation when the flag is absent. Shared structures remain blocking if any owner is active. Directional passage art faces the interior on opposite sides.

Spinner engagement records distinguish shared-only knowledge from personal contact. Shared-only crews remain eligible for unpaid idle recruitment, and an unfinished enclosure keeps its sole available builder. Positive native sampling classifies older saves without this marker. Native player recognition gates live pressure destinations; NPC awareness alone leaves only valid recorded coordinates. Existing paid-field ownership, native combat and Recovery remain authoritative.

## Special-floor population

`Population.prepareFloor(kind)` publishes the existing `KDMapData.SpiderlingsPopulationPlan` at the original theme hook point. The cap counts living mobile Spiderlings, including allies; it excludes nests and structures. Hunting Grounds uses the global numeric setting plus twenty, including zero becoming twenty, and does not retain an obsolete saved plan ceiling. The existing `getMapPopulationCap` and `availableSpiderlingSlots` names delegate to this module. `planCrews` is a read-only layout query and reserves nothing. `seedCrews` checks current capacity and revalidates selected cells before creating the required crews. Hunting Grounds requires three six-member crews and adds complete roaming crews while space and capacity remain; Infestation requires two crews across its five objectives. Hunting Grounds then places its separated native prey through `seedHuntingResidents` and fills remaining spider slots. Saved maps do not repeat this initial seeding.

Each required Hunting Grounds crew contains two Spinners, one Jumper, two WebCasters and one Mage. Each initial Infestation core crew contains one Spinner, one Jumper and one WebCaster. Failed creation removes this batch's births without death events and restores prior roster fields. If native summon inserts an actor and then throws, cleanup attributes only the pending birth at the requested name and cell, preserving unrelated hook births. Cleanup errors retain the original cause. An ordinary optional patrol failure leaves complete required crews active. Terrain carving, objective IDs, theme cancellation, escape state and rewards remain owned by the floor modules. No new save schema is introduced.

The floor modules retain their existing active-state timing. Native initial/wandering selectors remain in Hunting Grounds at their original wrapper positions; ordinary encounters and the optional squad keep their own placement rules. Recurring six-member nest reinforcement uses `missingRoles`, while saved four-member nests keep their legacy budget. Timers, probability, weights and lifetime Tunneler limits remain in Encounters. The implementation and verification record is [Test.87 population and hints](archive/validation/spiderlings-test87-population.zh-CN.md).

Hunting Grounds stores patrol membership on `entity.SpiderlingsHuntCrewID`; nest crews retain their parent ID. Its `crewContacts` records contain only target ID, observed coordinates and observation tick. Once per current map tick, the module groups living eligible spiders and resolves shared prey from actual native perception. Each actor still needs its own perception to acquire a live target; a recent report permits investigation of old coordinates only. Active nest defense overrides that investigation. This state stays inside the existing Hunting Grounds module and native movement hooks. WebMobility's cell-query memo exists only for a single synchronous weighted path search, so later searches reread changed locks and terrain.

## Shared native hooks

`Spiderlings.Hooks.wrap(owner, native, create)` preserves the supplied native function and returns a stable wrapper. Reinstalling an owner already present in that Spiderlings chain replaces its callback at the existing position. This keeps new callbacks aligned with newly registered events without accumulating wrappers. `describe(handler)` reports owners in installation order.

| Native function                | Owned hook                                                            | Delegation                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `KDAIType.*.beforemove`        | `NativeActions.beforemove.<type>`                                     | Shared duty/dispatch admission, Cocoon pre-native strategy, native movement, WebCaster post-native preference and contact/crew handling. |
| `KDAIType.*.aftermove`         | `NativeActions.aftermove.<type>`                                      | Native result followed by registered rivalry behavior when unhandled; preserves zero-time rules.                                         |
| `KDAIType.*.attack` / `.spell` | `NativeActions.attack/spell.<type>`                                   | The current action decision gates both native phases.                                                                                    |
| `KDGetDir`                     | `NativeActions.direction`, then the existing WebMobility adapter      | Native direction and registered species preference; later route adaptation retains precedence.                                           |
| `KinkyDungeonTrackSneak`       | `NativeActions.playerContact`                                         | Real positive-time player recognition at the native 0.5 threshold.                                                                       |
| `KinkyDungeonEnemyLoop`        | Existing Combat/NPCAdhesion observers, then `NativeActions.enemyLoop` | Ordered source controllers and field delegates; unhandled calls retain native context and arguments.                                     |
| `DrawButtonKDEx`               | `Webbing.boundWeaponIcon`                                             | Preserves the native button while omitting an unnamed weapon's nonexistent image.                                                        |

Each callback forwards the original receiver, arguments and result on its unhandled route. The registry tracks Spiderlings-owned wrappers only. It does not inspect or replace another Mod's private wrapper chain, and it is not a general-purpose Mod hot-reload system. Other compatibility hooks retain their existing owners and guards.

Capture admission is inspected without changing saved state. Native tick, movement, enemy and load events commit participant changes or interruption. Drawing can hide invalid previews, but cannot cancel capture, create retry timers, migrate fields or advance work. A lost contest creates no equipment. Each paid wrapping turn deposits 0.2 into the exact equipped item, and a source audit ends temporary control at zero sources without changing that item. The load handler audits schema-3 sources and the saved bag ID, reads deposited progress from the item and clears older capture state instead of inferring work. `phase()` and `isControllingPlayer()` expose read-only control state so recovery can pause without sharing source or leash ownership.

Player Recovery records connected Spinner sources without a fixed count limit and uses one native tether owner. Recovery admission requires the Spiderlings leg bag and a recorded successful crossing from inside the entire field to outside. A collar or usable leash only supplies a compatible carrier, not departure eligibility. Native equipment restrictions still apply. Additional eligible crew may pay to join through three-cell native LOS relays rooted at player contact. A pending departure with no usable anchor does not divert builders into futile pursuit. Each executor move uses native enemy movement; `KinkyDungeonAttachTetherToEntity` and `KinkyDungeonUpdateTether` own player displacement. Normal movement adds N−1 to native `MovePoints` debt for N sources, including above eight, and preserves existing slow costs. Sprint cannot bypass outstanding debt. The native restraint panel owns escape progress and carrier removal; the separate stand-firm and counted strand-removal actions are removed. The owned leash retains its 0.05 escape penalty per extra source. Borrowed leash contents remain unchanged, and an existing foreign native tether is never replaced. Core arrival releases only this recovery tether and preserves the carrier. Schema 3 now carries explicit `boundaryExit` evidence. Unproven older active/pending recovery is cleared on load without removing gear or changing item progress. Each completed return consumes its evidence.

NPC Recovery uses the same pending-source, source, executor, destination and crossing functions but owns no carrier or inventory event. Its read-only pursuit request uses the already selected native target and supplies no live target coordinates to AI. `enemyMove` records an actual departure through a breached outer field before field entry handling. A later native Spinner melee must add Slime before one allowed owner attaches. The executor spends one operation and uses `KDMoveEntity(target, x, y, false)` for the single legal displacement; native and owned `pulled` guards prevent another movement that turn. Returning to a repaired common core ends recovery but does not create Capture strands. Combat attempts normal NPC Capture first on every later positive-Slime hit and clears Recovery only after admission succeeds.

The public runtime-boundary tests cover rule-only execution, shared hook composition and rendering without state writes. The complete watcher adds native-source compatibility tests. ## Native Spinner field planning

The doorway line scenario remains a focused #28 fixture. Ordinary and infestation maps use the same paid builder operations after Rollout snapshots the map decision. Global planning uses current character positions and reachable workers without granting combat perception. Legal large layered enclosures take priority over small passage sites; native observations govern pursuit and attacks. A passage still must intercept an actual entrance-to-exit route through its capture interior, including suitable doorways, junctions and narrow corridors. An unavoidable route ranks above a route with a meaningful detour; merely facing the player or blocking adjacent mouths does not establish interception. Passage fallback still requires a valid route-interception proof. Existing autonomous line plans retire their owned topology and reselect a field; explicit authored line fixtures retain their behavior. Each handled construction pass performs one operation and rechecks terrain, protected metadata and actor occupancy.

The projection uses a non-immobile `SpiderlingsSpinnerTrap` enemy with `SpiderlingsWebTraversal` as its native path condition and the native `scenery` tag. KD 5.4.92 and the pinned reference apply swap admission after a successful path condition; the structural tag admits the Spiderling to the web cell. Ordinary enemies fail the condition and remain blocked. A Spiderling accepted by the condition enters the proxy cell without removing or swapping the web. One actual actor may stand on the web; another actor cannot enter that occupied cell. Reconciliation orders proxies before actors so native cached target lookup selects the actor when both share a cell. Direct web damage still requires the selected proxy identity. Projectiles and sight use the underlying floor tile. The adapter stores lines, passages and enclosures in the same schema-2 physical graph and reconciles exactly one proxy per solid cell. Native enemy drawing selects `SpiderlingsSpinnerTrapTop`, `Side` and `Corner` pieces from the declared perimeter, with camera-aware placement and Normal/Pink selection. The old WebCell name exists only for native save hydration; load reconciliation replaces it while retaining entity identity and field durability. Independent ground traps and their old tile triggers are retired. Current prepared field silk has its own native movement-delay rule described above.

Autonomous planning captures the map seed, forms groups from hostile capable Spinners within ten reachable steps and saves selected plans for travel. Nearby idle groups can merge into an existing field, prioritizing groups below two available workers. Merge distance measures the donor members' actual positions against the receiving site; a future plan center does not stand in for a distant worker. An uncommitted group may join a passage with fewer than four members from up to twenty reachable steps. Incoming helpers retain native travel and distinct rally/work reservations. Field space supplies distinct standby positions rather than a worker quota. Stations lie within two Chebyshev tiles of a gate, outside the interior, web boundary, protected or locked cells and other active planned web cells; a single-cell external gate approach stays clear. Live rally routing still checks reachability and occupancy. Crew members remain assigned to their invested or completed field, including when waiting space is scarce. A donor with paid construction, preparation or surviving built structures cannot merge away from its field. Before paid work begins, a new recognized observation can redirect an approach after at least four coordination turns: the target changed, moved at least four tiles from the planning observation, or reversed its recorded direction. The same crew replaces only that unpaid plan. Observation changes do not relocate invested or completed fields; actual invalid terrain still follows the existing invalidation path. Capture, Recovery, wrapping and task-nest defense keep their priority. No entity is removed or teleported. Each transition into `ready` recalculates waiting-gate assignments once from current member positions, then keeps them stable. Gate allocation accounts for the remaining uncovered mouths. Membership changes or a saved partition made unreachable by a stationed coworker trigger repartition; ordinary waiting does not. If coworkers prevent a complete actor-avoiding route, an incoming worker may approach along the free prefix of the native terrain route; the next-step occupancy check still prevents passing through any actor. Capture, recovery and nest defense prevent reassignment, and task-nest ownership restrictions remain in effect. Consolidation reduces duplicate fields; new independent deployments also obey the configured limit and Spinner population permits.

Candidate analysis reads floor topology, protected and locked metadata, entrances, exits, native walls, actor occupancy and member origins. `SpiderlingsSpinnerPassagePlanner.js` shares static connectivity, bounded route proofs and cached distances across groups and turns; terrain or relevant metadata changes invalidate that analysis. No-site results wait for a geometry or relevant occupancy change before retrying. Rollout records the selected passage or enclosure without replacing topology. One actionable Spinner may continue an authorized field's construction, while new deployment also requires its population permit. Capture needs two legal sources and a real native hit. See [passage algorithms and proof limits](spinner-passage-algorithms.md).

`afterDamageEnemy.dmgDealt` is the only durability input. Reconciliation then overwrites surviving proxy HP from topology and removes breached cells. Every solid-cell change sets `KDUpdateEnemyCache` and replaces both `KDPathCache` and `KDPathCacheIgnoreLocks`; `KDUpdateDoorNavMap()` alone is insufficient for the second cache in this patch. `afterLoadGame` reuses one valid proxy per cell, drops duplicates and creates missing projections without changing saved HP or ownership. Positive `tickAfter` events on the active map alone advance the final-owner collapse age.

Mobile actors tagged `spiderlings` or `spider` receive 1.5 times ordinary movement credit only when their live destination is an intact Spiderlings-owned web. Credit remains fractional across attempts; a blocked move cannot earn the extra credit, and leaving the web uses ordinary movement. No MoveSpeed buff is applied, so attack, spell, construction and wrapping budgets retain their own cadence. Spider pursuit uses a separate actor-aware weighted route with 2/3 base cost for owned-web steps, including visible-target direction choice. Web changes, actual actor moves and load clear its cache. Other actors and unrelated webs retain native routing.

## Native passage fields

A passage saves its interior, grouped gate cells and borrowed native-wall cells in the existing schema-2 graph. Its rectangular interior is one to three cells per side, with two to four straight mouths. The complete eight-neighbor perimeter must consist of declared gates or actual native walls, including diagonal escape routes. Native doors, protected objects and unknown obstacles cannot serve as borrowed walls or web cells. The adapter rechecks the live terrain before paid work and capture admission. Losing a borrowed wall makes the field breached without creating a replacement.

All passage gates start open. Each gate takes a paid preparation action, which produces a faint Normal/Pink drawing only; no blocking or damageable entity is created. Prey entering the declared interior arms closure. Workers pay one action for each gate cell and another to connect each complete gate. Occupied cells wait. Only a fully closed valid field can admit a later qualifying melee hit. While sealed, support workers share the group's native last-known position only while it is less than four turns old and inside the field. They physically move toward nearby unoccupied cells, including gate and interior cells, without the waiting-gate partition limiting their approach. This movement does not grant capture sources or replace native melee admission. Target departure disarms closure and schedules paid reopening of the designated travel gates.

While prey remains outside and closure is not armed, at least two gates are designated open. A native observation aged less than four turns can choose the entrance nearest the last-known approach and a separate through gate toward the floor exit. Adjustments require a ready, undamaged field, no engaged capture/recovery sources and at least eight turns since the last change. Newly required openings are physically reopened before other mouths may close. Selection alone never places, removes or heals web cells. Broken gates retain their four-positive-turn rebuild cooldown; rebuilding and repair still consume actions. A saved entry or departure change cannot keep an obsolete close/reopen job alive.

Old single-entrance enclosures keep their geometry, construction and HP when loaded. Passage state adds gates and native-wall references without migrating them into a different field shape. Graphical objects, path caches and the static passage index stay outside saves. This ordinary test feature is separate from the `prison.alpha.N` experiment.

## Native enclosing-field slice

Issue #92 uses the same map-owned topology and `SpiderlingsSpinnerTrap` projection as the doorway line. Every declared enclosure is a saved simple orthogonal polygon. New autonomous enclosure candidates try radii four, three, two and one for legal 9-by-9, 7-by-7, 5-by-5 and 3-by-3 outer footprints. All selected concentric layers are declared together, with `constructionOrder: "outer-first"`; saved layer IDs remain inner to outer and the radius-two inner ring provides a 3-by-3 common core; only the smallest fallback has a one-cell core. Saved existing cores remain authoritative. One owner may build and close the field; every segment, closure and repair consumes a paid action. Existing invested rings remain in place and can gain legal outer layers without rebuilding their completed cells. New autonomous rings leave aligned gates open until prey enters the common core. Existing auto-sealed fields retain their sealed-inner-ring expansion rules. Player Capture strands retain their separate two-source admission requirement and use only the inner interior cell for a minimum field.

The physical graph expands boundaries to unit edges, merges equal edges and coalesces collinear spans only while owner sets match. Original polygon vertices remain real anchors. Overlap splits and crossings are junctions without anchor HP. Identical and partial overlaps therefore share one HP pool per physical span and one native proxy per occupied cell.

For an outer-first composite, builders complete an outer body before selecting work in the next smaller inner body. `nextWorkAction` reverses only construction priority; reopening, containment, capture core and saved layer numbering retain their existing meaning. Each autonomous ring retains one aligned entrance into the common core. A living hostile player or non-Spiderling NPC inside that core arms only that composite's closure; nearby actor movement does not cancel it. Closing each gate requires one paid cell operation and one paid connection operation. Once prey leaves or disappears, paid reopening restores the entrances, including partly closed gates. Completed body cells remain. Declared containment, outside-field reachability, outside-composite reachability and the route to the floor exit are separate queries. A crossing without a declared polygon is never a capture field.

Live owners may spend one action to restore 10 percent of a damaged structure's maximum HP. A destroyed anchor or link waits four positive active-map turns before paid rebuilding. Retired-only work is not repaired. The final valid owner still starts the 20-turn collapse count. `afterLoadGame` restores partly built, sealed and breached graphs and reconciles proxies idempotently.

Debug scenarios use the same enclosure and controller runtime as Rollout. They admit Capture strands only after a qualifying native Spinner hit; field damage after admission does not cancel those strands. Scenario control saves selected actor IDs, target ID, awareness and the pre-existing encounter so a real load can resume controls and teardown restores unrelated state.

## Native lure-coordination slice
